# Before V18: uncertainty and productive deployment

These public-state audits precede the [V18 hypothesis](v18-plan.md). They run
no new games and establish no improvement. The completed V17 screen remains
rejected; the competitive objective remains unmet.

## Remembered pressure is an unresolved allocation problem

V3 already tried persistent enemy-threat estimates and held home reserves.
Its audited loss retained hypothetical force after a visible battle depleted
both armies, reversed a convoy, and accumulated 108 of 272 armies at home by
turn 500 while the opponent had 536. It lost at 665 where V2 won at 383.
The broader V3 evaluation was also runtime-confounded and unpromoted. Simply
restoring a remembered maximum would repeat a tested regression risk.

The V17 Amin loss and winning V10 control both observe enemy force 55 at 169,
then visible depletion. Both halve home shortly afterward, at 173 and 174
respectively. By 185 the visible maximum has fallen to two and three. V17's
newly visible force 64 at 224 cannot be identified as the earlier army through
fog. Holding the old maximum or vetoing every donation would also change the
winner. Static gathering at 224 is too weak along one examined route, but a
loose instantaneous donor upper bound does not establish global impossibility.

The Juraj control likewise supplies concrete reasons to release defenders:
one released packet contributes to a castle at 488; another screens and builds
at 809. A late home sortie leaves two armies before an actual attack, yet the
control survives and wins at 1075. These are observed preservation witnesses,
not proof that every action was necessary. V17's smaller early economy and
late insufficient screen require more than a permanent reserve.

## Scoreboard uncertainty does not discriminate

Subtracting visible enemy army from its public total gives an upper bound on
unseen army, not a localized attacker. Subtracting one garrison per other hidden
tile would tighten it only under an unsupported assumption: equal combat and
exact-cost construction can leave owned tiles with zero armies. Actual Amin
observations contain such tiles.

Across 4,338 original Amin frames, ordinary fog remains two terrain steps from
home. Across 5,244 Juraj frames, the unconditional unseen-army bound exceeds
home army at every postcontact frame in all four arms, including all 999 of
the winning control's postcontact frames. Even the conditional minimum-garrison
formula triggers on 994 of those 999. These scalar alarms do not isolate the
losses or choose a useful reserve. Public terrain distance ignores armies and
does not identify an actual hidden route.

## Separate-garrison capture opportunities

A second census examines 4,791 original decisions across the first mirrors of
V10, V10-v6, V16 and V17 against Amin and Juraj. A local alternative uses a
different ordinary owned two-army tile to capture adjacent visible ordinary
neutral zero, with no neighboring visible enemy transferable surplus at either
endpoint. The conservative availability tier excludes visible defense/general
priorities and requires both incoming/returned offensive phases zero and both
defender slots uncommitted.

| Opponent | V10 | V10-v6 | V16 | V17 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Amin postcontact opportunities | 167 | 101 | 46 | 11 |
| Juraj postcontact opportunities | 60 | 321 | 31 | 178 |

These counts precede the final V18 predicates and home-safety check. They are
availability on trajectories of different lengths, not activation counts,
independent strength samples or confirmed captures. Winning controls expose
many opportunities too. Replacing a transfer preserves its packet's army while
delaying its movement, and a two-army donor may itself have future value.

V14's service ledger could repay debt through another owned transfer; V15's
cheap neutral expansion stopped permanently after first contact. V18 tests a
narrower postcontact capture action on an uncommitted V10 transfer. Its full
games must establish whether durable production repays the action cost.
